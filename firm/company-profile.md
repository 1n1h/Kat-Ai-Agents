## Scope of practice

1. **Commercial litigation** — antitrust, breach of contract, construction,
   real estate, securities, trademark infringement, banking/creditor
   disputes, professional liability, toxic torts. Business clients;
   damages theory often includes lost future profits. House message:
   act timely — "delaying the exercise of your rights could mean losing them."
2. **Civil trial practice** — state and federal courts across Florida;
   100+ jury trials in firm history, including a six-month Miami-Dade
   trial and a three-month S.D. Fla. trial.
3. **Insurance litigation** — **defense side: the firm represents insurers
   and self-insured corporations, not policyholders.** First- and
   third-party claims, subrogation, coverage disputes, bad-faith
   litigation, claim management. Heavy hurricane-claim experience
   (1,000+ Irma/Michael cases). Strong preference for resolving through
   mediation/arbitration where possible.
4. **Products liability litigation** — including subrogation/recovery work
   for corporate clients.
5. **Equine law** — a signature niche: horse sale/breeding/stallion
   contracts, mortality/injury insurance claims, farm employment
   disputes; clients are owners, breeders, and equine professionals
   (Ocala/Wellington corridor). The firm has deep personal ties to the
   industry (board seat on the National Show Horse Registry; a national
   champion equestrian on the team).
6. **Mediation & arbitration** — Phillip (Florida Certified Circuit and
   County Court Mediator; Qualified Arbitrator) and Johanna (Certified
   Circuit and County Court Mediator) take neutral engagements in personal
   injury, commercial, products, insurance, and equine disputes.
7. **Corporate counseling** — advising clients on internal systems that
   reduce or prevent legal exposure.

## How the firm works (for agent behavior)

- Litigation posture is usually **defense** (especially insurance);
  commercial work is often plaintiff-side recovery for businesses.
- Culture prizes **meticulous preparation** and trial readiness, but
  resolves through ADR when it serves the client.
- Florida law is the default jurisdiction; federal practice is common
  (S.D. Fla., M.D. Fla., 11th Circuit).
- When drafting or analyzing, watch for insurer-side framing in coverage
  and bad-faith matters, and equine-industry custom in horse matters.
